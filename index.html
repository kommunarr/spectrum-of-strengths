Heritage · systems · visionary leadership

We ARE the Spectrum.

Spectrum of Strengths is building a public record rooted in dignity, heritage, and the strengths of neurodivergent communities, including autistic and ADHD people.

We celebrate history and tradition, identify gaps boldly, and explore evidence-led products and changes that bridge unmet needs and create shared value.

Four ways of thinking about value

These four ideas guide how we look at needs, solutions, and shared benefit.

Value capture

Recognize and share the economic and social benefits created by people’s knowledge, work, and relationships, so value reaches the communities who help create it and supports lasting work.

Value transformation

Improve existing knowledge, services, relationships, or resources so they work better for people and communities.

Value creation

Develop new ideas, tools, products, or services in response to a real need.

Value preservation

Protect useful knowledge, practices, and relationships so they can continue to benefit people over time.

From a need to shared value

Our living lab will connect evidence of a need with a proposed response and the value it could create. These are questions to examine, not promised outcomes.

The need

Document the gap, who it affects, and the evidence behind it.

A possible response

Describe a product, service, or practical change that could bridge the gap.

Potential shared value

Explain the possible social benefit and economic return, including who could receive and sustain that value.

Archive & journal

A record in progress

The archive and journal will grow as research, lived experience, historical material, and organizational progress are prepared for publication. Building trust with national bodies and professionals takes time; we will share confirmed milestones when they are ready for the public record. This first stage sets the direction and does not claim that services or programs are already operating.

Spectrum of Strengths opens its public record

Spectrum of Strengths is opening a public record rooted in heritage, history, tradition, dignity, and the strengths of neurodivergent communities, including autistic and ADHD people. We intend to bring research and lived experience together in an archive and living lab, identify unmet needs, and develop practical responses that create and share value. Our plans include products and future community centres. These are intentions under development; no community centres or programs are currently operating.
