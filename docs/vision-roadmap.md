# Vision-aligned work catalog

Updated 2026-09-28. This is a planning companion to [remaining work](remaining-work.md)
and the [first-stage decisions](first-stage-decisions.md). It compares the
published bilingual site with the creator's original direction. It is a
catalog of opportunities, not a commitment to announce services or publish
unreviewed claims.

## Where the site stands

The first release establishes the name, “We ARE the Spectrum” identity,
heritage and systems focus, four value ideas, Foundations, plain-language key
terms, an Archive & journal, and English/French routes. Home and Archive each
have an automatically changing, gently fading information carousel with small
controls and a static layout for reduced-motion visitors. The archive has one
approved opening record with a dated, shareable bilingual page. Events and
Contact are clearly in development; there are no submission or payment flows.

The largest difference between this foundation and the creator's vision is
the depth of the public record. The historical archive, documented social
needs, proposed responses, strengths, and establishment journal still need
source-backed entries. The site should grow around that material rather than
adding empty sections or complex tools ahead of it.

## Priority 1: Make the public record substantive

| Opportunity | Next useful step | What must come from the organization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical archive about heritage and systems | Prepare the first source-led bilingual entry with a date, context, a plain-language explanation of why the record matters, and a stable source link where public. Connect it to the relevant Foundations idea. | One public source or a safe description of a private record, plus permission to share the proposed facts. |
| Living journal of establishment and due process | Publish short dated updates when a real milestone, plan, or status change can be described. Separate the date of a source from the date an entry is published; label plans, work in progress, and confirmed milestones accurately. | Rough notes or a source reference, the source date if known, and confirmation of the public wording. Private correspondence stays outside the repository. |
| Strengths and lived experience | Prepare an entry format that celebrates a specific strength or contribution and explains its context without reducing people to a diagnosis or inventing outcomes. | A willing contributor, their account, and explicit approval of the name, quotation, image, and scope of publication, if any. Anonymous summaries remain possible. |
| Evidence of a gap beside a response and value | Use the existing optional *documented need / proposed response / potential shared value* fields for the first supported case. State who may benefit and who might sustain or share the economic value; keep untested returns conditional. | Evidence for the need and enough detail to describe the proposed response accurately. Confirm any named people or institutions. |
| Four value ideas made concrete | As cases accumulate, link each value idea to a real record showing capture, transformation, creation, or preservation in practice. Keep the plain-language definitions concise. | Supported examples and approval of any organizational claims. |

The existing [archive entry workflow](archive-entry-workflow.md) is the handoff:
the owner can send rough notes or a link. The maintainer chooses the category,
drafts and translates the entry, checks the source, and asks for a focused
review of facts and permissions. No new intake system is required.

## Priority 2: Help visitors follow the story

These are design and editorial improvements the maintainer can prepare as the
record grows. They should be driven by actual entries and visitor needs.

| Opportunity | Suggested trigger and scope |
| --- | --- |
| A clearer path through the archive | With several entries, add short introductions and related-entry links from a historical source to a present need, proposed response, and later update. Keep a direct path to the complete record for readers who prefer to browse at their own pace. |
| Timeline and source context | When there are enough dated records, offer a simple chronological view. Show publication dates separately from source dates and explain whether an entry is a plan, work in progress, or a confirmed event. Do not infer causes or endorsements from a timestamp alone. |
| Findability | Add category filters or search only when the archive is large enough that scrolling no longer works well. Keep every record reachable by a normal link and preserve its stable URL. |
| Richer but calm visual storytelling | Use approved historical images, diagrams, or documents to make heritage and systems visible. Connect the carousels to real records when suitable; refine pacing or layout using actual visitor feedback. Keep pause and keyboard controls, reduced-motion static content, and readable text. |
| Foundations with substance | Expand Origins, Contributors, Purpose, Relationships, and Embodiment as supporting facts arrive. The current introductory text is an honest starting point; named profiles and milestones need review. |
| Language and terminology | Continue paired Canadian English/French editing. Define specialized framework terms where they help understanding; avoid filling the glossary with ordinary words merely for completeness. Review proper names and organization-specific French wording with a knowledgeable person when needed. |

The carousel interaction should continue to follow the [W3C WAI carousel
pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/carousel/): automatic rotation
has a visible stop/start control and stops for keyboard focus and pointer
hover. The creator's wish for an organic, self-guided experience should guide
the visual treatment while leaving direct navigation available.

## Priority 3: Preserve credibility as the archive grows

- Keep a private source inventory outside the public repository: original
  record, source date, rights or consent, safe public citation, and the public
  entries that refer to it. Back up the originals separately from the site.
  Library and Archives Canada's [digital preservation guidance for small
  museums](https://www.canada.ca/en/heritage-information-network/services/digital-preservation/recommendations-small-museums.html)
  offers useful principles for provenance and preservation; it is a reference,
  not a requirement imposed on this organization.
- Maintain a concise correction path for dated entries: fix errors visibly
  where the correction affects meaning, preserve the original source record,
  and avoid silently changing what a dated claim appeared to say. The
  maintainer can draft this convention before the archive has many entries.
- Continue the quarterly content review already assigned in the operational
  backlog. Check current-state claims, broken source links, translations,
  accessibility, and whether development notices still describe reality.
- Keep the present static, Git-backed publishing workflow while one technical
  maintainer can manage updates. Revisit a browser editor only when publication
  frequency or multiple editors make it a real time saver. Preview, translation
  pairing, role ownership, rollback, and draft privacy then become requirements.

## Later capabilities, each with a readiness trigger

| Capability | Add when | Minimum before launch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public resources or research library | There is a curated set of approved sources that people need to browse separately from journal entries. | Source rights, context, language availability, maintenance owner, and a way to report stale links. |
| Public events and registration | A real event and organizer are ready. | Confirmed date, location or access details, capacity and cancellation process, attendee-data handling, and bilingual information. Show information only if registration is not needed. |
| Contact and accessibility feedback | Someone can reliably receive and answer messages. | Approved public destination or provider, response expectations, spam controls, and updated privacy wording. |
| Volunteering, donations, or mailing list | The organization is operationally ready to receive people, funds, or recurring communications. | An owner for each workflow, actual fulfillment process, provider account, consent or payment handling, and matching public policy text. |
| Community-centre or cross-border information | Plans become specific and shareable, or services actually open. | Confirmed status, scope, dates, location, responsible organization, and approval of any partner or operational claim. |
| Custom domain, analytics, or CMS | A concrete communications or maintenance need makes the extra service worthwhile. | Account and billing ownership, privacy implications, migration/rollback plan, and benefit to visitors or maintainers. |

## Claims needing a focused source review

The maintainer can prepare a short draft when source material arrives. The
creator need only verify the factual statement and sharing permission; routine
layout, categories, and translation can be handled without further decisions.

1. The exact wording and context of any Public Health Agency of Canada review
   or “ambitious” description. Do not imply endorsement from the creator's
   account alone.
2. Names, roles, and present status of national bodies, professionals,
   contributors, and US organizations. Distinguish conversations from
   partnerships and future intentions from operational work.
3. Any account of institutional barriers or delays: dated records, careful
   attribution, and review of criticism before public release. A timestamp
   supports when correspondence existed, not the reason for a delay.
4. Historical sources, personal stories, and images: provenance, usage rights,
   consent, accessibility description, and any details that must stay private.
5. Specific products, community-centre plans, economic returns, and social
   outcomes: describe proposals as proposals until there is evidence of actual
   implementation or measured results.

## Suggested order of execution

1. Ask for one safe, rough source note for the first historical or progress
   entry, whichever is ready first. The maintainer drafts both languages and
   a narrow factual review packet.
2. Publish a small sequence of real entries that demonstrates heritage,
   strengths, need, proposed response, and progress. Link related records once
   they exist.
3. Revisit archive browsing and visual storytelling using those entries.
4. Add operational features only when their owners and real workflows are in
   place. Keep routine site maintenance and accessibility review running in
   parallel.
